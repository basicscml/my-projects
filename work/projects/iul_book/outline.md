# Bulletproof Retirement — IUL Book

_Working outline. Draft v0.1 — starter scaffold for review, not client-ready copy._

> **Compliance note:** This book discusses Indexed Universal Life (IUL)
> insurance, a regulated financial product. Nothing in this outline is final or
> approved. All copy must pass licensed/legal/compliance review before any use
> with the public. See `shared/memory/global_working_agreement.md`.

## Audience

Pre-retirees and mid-career earners (roughly 40–60) who want tax-advantaged
accumulation, downside protection, and a death benefit — and who are skeptical
of "too good to be true" pitches.

## Promise of the book

Explain, honestly and plainly, how an IUL can fit into a retirement plan: what it
does well, what it costs, where it can go wrong, and who it is (and is not) for.

## Part I — Why retirement feels fragile

1. The three big risks: market loss, longevity, taxes.
2. Why "save and pray" in the market alone leaves a gap.
3. What "bulletproof" really means here: reducing specific risks, not eliminating
   all risk. (Set honest expectations early.)

## Part II — IUL fundamentals

4. What an IUL actually is: permanent life insurance + an index-linked
   accumulation account.
5. How crediting works: index options, **caps, participation rates, floors,
   and spreads** — and that these can change.
6. The floor (often 0%) and what it does and does not protect.
7. Costs that are easy to miss: cost of insurance, admin/policy fees, rider
   charges, surrender charges.

## Part III — The retirement use case

8. Tax treatment: tax-deferred growth and the mechanics of policy loans.
9. Designing for income vs. designing for death benefit — the trade-off.
10. Funding levels and the danger of an underfunded policy (lapse risk, MEC
    rules).
11. Illustrations: how to read one, and why illustrated rates are **not**
    guarantees.

## Part IV — Honest comparisons

12. IUL vs. 401(k)/IRA — different jobs, not direct substitutes.
13. IUL vs. term + invest the difference.
14. IUL vs. whole life and fixed annuities.

## Part V — Who it's for / who it isn't

15. Good-fit profiles and red-flag profiles.
16. Questions to ask any agent before you sign.
17. Common sales tactics to be wary of.

## Part VI — Putting it together

18. A sample planning framework (illustrative only).
19. Working with a licensed professional.
20. Next steps and a plain-language glossary.
